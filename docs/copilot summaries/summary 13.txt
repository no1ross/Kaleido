1. Core Requirements

Kaleido.Process is not a workflow engine.

It is a metadata-driven and business-driven orchestration framework.
The runtime/orchestrator should remain relatively "stupid" and delegate decision making to specialized services (planner, evaluator, invoker, state updater, registry, etc.).

The framework is intended to support a multi-BU enterprise architecture:

External consumers call a common API layer.
A process orchestrator routes requests to the appropriate BU.
Each BU owns one or more products.
Each product owns its own participant handlers, business rules, persistence, and internal data.
The orchestration layer should be independent from product-specific business logic.

Process steps are effectively business capability contracts.

A step receives a request.
A step executes business logic.
A step returns a response plus process guidance/messages.

Every step should have a response contract.

The original request-only model is no longer sufficient.
Responses are considered part of the public contract.
Even steps with no meaningful payload should return a framework-provided empty response type.

Future endpoint/OpenAPI generation strongly benefits from explicit request/response contracts.

Overall Architectural Outcome

The largest decision made in this conversation:

Kaleido.Process has formally evolved from a request-only execution model into a request/response orchestration framework.

Key characteristics:

Every step has a response.
Responses are contracts.
Responses are not owned by Process state.
Response type metadata belongs in registration.
Business data remains owned by participant services.
Process remains an orchestration framework, not a workflow engine.
